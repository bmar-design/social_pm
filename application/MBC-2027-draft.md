## 1 Problem and opportunity

Sports fans share predictions with friends, but the conversation and the action often happen in separate places. A pick posted in a group chat can lose context as messages accumulate, and friends have no consistent way to respond, track their own positions, or revisit the group's predictions. Our initial hypothesis is that fans want a private, shared experience that connects sports conversation with clear, individual decisions about each prediction. We are starting with college football and existing friend groups. [Add evidence from customer interviews or observed behavior.]

## 2 Competition

Our initial competitive hypothesis is that customers combine group messaging, sports information, and standalone betting or prediction-market apps. Messaging keeps friends connected but does not organize predictions into structured decisions and position histories. Transaction-focused apps organize individual activity, but customers may still coordinate with friends elsewhere. We aim to bring that interaction into one private group experience. [Validate through customer interviews and a comparison of specific competing products before submitting.]

## 3 Solution

Social Plays is a mobile social prediction app where friends discuss games, share a prediction, and independently choose to Tail the proposed outcome or Fade it by choosing the opposite outcome. Users review their own amount and explicitly confirm each practice play. Private groups combine conversation, reactions, and shared picks, while personal profiles organize positions and history. The prototype also supports sharing and remixing multi-pick combinations. Our proposed differentiation is making the friend group the center of the prediction experience. Current trading is simulated; live-money execution, funding, and payouts remain future integrations.

## 4 One sentence description

Social Plays is a sports prediction app that lets friends discuss games, share picks, and track their own predictions in private groups.

## 6 Business model

Our initial hypothesis is a freemium consumer model: free access to core private-group features, with a paid tier for enhanced group tools and personal performance analysis. We will test willingness to pay alongside repeat group engagement. Transaction-based revenue is a separate potential model that depends on execution-provider arrangements and the eventual launch model; it is not an established revenue stream.
